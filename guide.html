1Managed RL: a model learns Wordle

A 27B Qwen model learns to play Wordle with reinforcement learning (GRPO) on Crusoe Managed RL. Managed RL is in private preview.

What's on screen

Three Wordle boards race on the same secret word: Base Model, After SFT, After RL. The RL board wins most of the time. Underneath, the training run replays: the SFT loss curve, then the win rate climbing across 60 GRPO steps. The tiles on the left sum it up: base 2%, after SFT 22%, after RL 58%.

Say this

The base model guesses almost at random. Supervised fine-tuning teaches it the format. RL is what teaches it to actually win: it plays thousands of games, gets a reward for winning, and GRPO turns that reward into better guesses.

You write the game and the reward in Python. Crusoe runs the GPUs, the rollouts and the training, and hands you back a checkpoint you own.

Wordle is the toy. The same loop trains agents, tool use, coding, anything you can score.

The tabs

  • 1 Watch is the attract loop.
  • 2 How To Train shows what you write versus what Crusoe runs.
  • 3 GRPO Under The Hood walks one training step in six stations.
  • 4 Get Started has the QR codes: the developer hub on GitHub, the console, Discord.

Controls

Space pauses, R resets, Esc goes back to Watch, F is full screen. Left alone for three minutes it returns to Watch on its own.

If asked

The boards replay real games from the real models. The win-rate curve is illustrative, shaped on the real run's evaluations. The SFT loss curve is the real run.

2fastokens vs HuggingFace tokenizers

fastokens is a tokenizer that drops in for HuggingFace tokenizers and is several times to about 85 times faster on a single prompt. The gap grows as prompts get longer.

What's on screen

One chart. Grey line: HuggingFace. Yellow line: fastokens. The shaded band between them is the difference, and it opens up left to right as documents get bigger. The big number on the right is the speedup on the document that just landed. It cycles through five model tokenizers (gpt-oss-120b, DeepSeek-V3.2, MiniMax-M2.1, Mistral-Nemo, NVIDIA Nemotron-3-Nano) on two datasets, twenty documents per pass, about twenty seconds each.

Say this

Tokenizing is the first thing an inference server does on every request, and it sits in front of the GPU.

HuggingFace tokenizes one piece at a time, so twice the text is twice the wait. fastokens splits a long prompt across cores. Same documents, same machine, one at a time.

Long context is where it bites: a million-token prompt is hundreds of milliseconds before the GPU even starts.

Controls

Space, or the Pause button, holds the current pass so you can talk over it. That is the only control.

If asked

The numbers come from one recorded sweep, measured once and replayed here. Nothing is measured on the booth machine, so it cannot drift.

3Managed Slurm: topology-aware scheduling

Crusoe Managed Slurm discovers the network topology automatically and keeps topology.conf up to date. The GPU fleet on screen is simulated; the scheduler is Slurm's real topology/tree plugin, unmodified.

What's on screen

A hall of 10 racks, 8 nodes each, 8 GPUs per node. 512 GPUs are in service; the two dark racks at the end are installed but not yet switched on. Each rack has a leaf switch above it, with two spine switches above those. Job colours are the whole story: yellow sits on one leaf switch (fastest), amber spans leaves under one spine, red spans both spine planes (slowest). On the right: the topology.conf panel, the queue, and the scheduler log. Bottom left: the same job, 6.9 hours well placed versus 12 hours badly placed.

Say this

Slurm can only place a job well if it knows the wiring. On most clouds you cannot see the wiring, so topology.conf is a hand-written guess that goes stale.

Crusoe generates it from the real fabric, and regenerates it whenever the fleet changes.

Then press + Add capacity:

Two racks come online. topology.conf regenerates by itself, the queued jobs start on the new racks, and the hand-written config is now blind to 128 GPUs.

Controls

  • Launch a job: pick a GPU count, press sbatch, watch where it lands.
  • Pause and Resume. Restart starts the run over.
  • + Add capacity, once per run. The set-piece.
  • Autoplay runs the whole story on a loop every 18 seconds: the mode for an unattended screen.
  • Drag or scroll to move the camera; it drifts back on its own.

If asked

The placement decisions are real Slurm logic. The hardware, the jobs and the clock are simulated.
